\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiment}

This section reports the five experiment families that the matrix of Section~\ref{sec:our_work} supports. Each family answers a specific question about the parser, and the families are ordered from the established Stage-1 baselines to the new Stage-2 findings.

\subsection{Experimental protocol}
\label{sec:exp-protocol}

All runs share an identical training harness so that any accuracy gap can be attributed to the component under study. The training set contains 8\,301 sentences with on average 30 tokens per sentence; the development set contains 534 sentences with 12\,987 evaluable arcs after punctuation filtering. We use gradient accumulation to reach an effective batch size of 128 and clip gradients to an $\ell_2$ norm of nine for the BiLSTM runs and to no clipping for the Muon-trained Transformer runs, following the recommendations of \citet{dozat2017biaffine} and \citet{jordan2024muon} respectively. Word embeddings come from the Chinese-Word-Vectors collection \citep{li2018cwv}; unless explicitly varied, we use the mixed-large 300-dimensional source. The pretrained embedding table is frozen. Reported numbers are unlabeled attachment score (UAS) and labeled attachment score (LAS) on the development set, computed by the same evaluator used in the Stage-1 baselines so that the numbers are directly comparable. Each run is reported as a single seed because the within-seed variance of UAS on this corpus is below 0.2 on the runs we re-executed and is therefore much smaller than the cross-condition gaps we discuss.

\subsection{A. Word vector dimensionality}
\label{sec:exp-dim}

The first experiment family asks whether reducing the dimensionality of the pretrained embeddings hurts the parser. Because the Chinese-Word-Vectors release ships only 300-dimensional vectors, we generate a 100-dimensional comparison via post-hoc PCA on the same source corpus, retaining 73.5\% of the original variance. The two configurations differ only in the dimensionality of the frozen pretrained embedding table and in the corresponding scalar parameter \texttt{embed\_dim} that selects between 300 and 100. The 300-dimensional run reaches 86.43 dev UAS and the 100-dimensional PCA-reduced run reaches 86.14 dev UAS, a gap of 0.29 in favor of the higher dimensionality. The key reading of this result is not that dimensionality is irrelevant but that two thirds of the encoded variance can be discarded for a 0.3-point cost, suggesting that the directions of high variance in the embedding space are well aligned with the parsing-relevant directions.

\subsection{B. Word vector corpus domain}
\label{sec:exp-domain}

The second experiment family fixes the dimensionality at 300 and varies the source corpus over four domain-distinct collections: mixed-large, Renmin Ribao (newspaper), Zhihu (Q\&A) and a literature-only release. The four configurations reach 86.43, 86.38, 86.29 and 86.23 dev UAS respectively. The maximum gap of 0.20 UAS is below what we consider a substantive difference for an 8K-sentence treebank, and it is reassuring that the only configuration that drops more than one UAS is the from-scratch baseline of Section~\ref{sec:exp-pretrain} which uses no pretrained vectors at all. The conclusion is that, for this parser and this treebank, the domain of the source corpus that produced the pretrained vectors is essentially irrelevant as long as the corpus is large enough to provide reasonable coverage of the in-domain vocabulary. The pretrained-vs.-random axis is therefore much more important than the pretrained-domain axis, a finding we did not anticipate.

\subsection{C. Optimizers}
\label{sec:exp-optimizer}

The third experiment family fixes the encoder at the BiLSTM and the word vectors at mixed-large 300d and varies the optimizer through three configurations. The Adam baseline reaches 86.43 dev UAS in 50 epochs. The SGD baseline, configured with learning rate $10^{-2}$ and no momentum, reaches 17.66 dev UAS---essentially the random-guessing accuracy---and confirms that the LSTM/Biaffine combination requires an adaptive optimizer. The Muon optimizer reaches 85.51 dev UAS in the same 50-epoch budget but, after the schedule is extended to 100 epochs with a warmup--cosine schedule, climbs to 86.93 dev UAS and surpasses the Adam baseline by 0.50 UAS. We read this trio of numbers as evidence that Muon converges to a slightly better optimum than Adam on this combination of model and data, but that it does so on a longer time scale: at 50 epochs the orthogonalized updates have not yet completed their late-stage micro-adjustments, while at 100 epochs they have. This pattern is consistent with the observations of \citet{jordan2024muon} on small-scale language model training, where Muon's advantage materializes precisely when the training budget is long enough for the orthogonalization step to refine the late-stage update directions.

\subsection{D. Encoder family}
\label{sec:exp-encoder}

The fourth experiment family is the central new contribution of the paper and asks whether the choice of contextual encoder is the bottleneck of the parser on this data scale. We compare three encoder families at increasing parameter budgets, holding the optimizer fixed at Muon with the appropriate per-encoder learning rate as discussed below.

\paragraph{The matched-budget Transformer (post-norm) fails completely.} A four-layer Transformer with 9M parameters, sinusoidal positional encoding and the post-norm residual layout of \citet{vaswani2017attention} reaches 12.20 dev UAS with Adam and 53.35 dev UAS with Muon. Training-side UAS confirms that the Adam configuration never learns the task: the running training accuracy remains in the 1--3\% range for the entire run, never reaching the accuracy that a trivial always-attach-to-root baseline would achieve. We diagnose this failure as a textbook post-norm-without-warmup collapse \citep{xiong2020lnorder}: the first few large-norm gradient steps move the network into a region from which the post-norm residual structure cannot recover. The Muon result on the same encoder is more interesting. While Muon also fails to reach a competitive UAS, it nevertheless climbs to 53 UAS before retreating to 43 UAS by the time the early-stopping patience is exhausted. We interpret the 53 UAS as a consequence of the implicit gradient clipping that the Newton--Schulz orthogonalization provides, which we proved formally in Section~\ref{sec:theory}: even when the raw gradients would cause the post-norm residual stream to diverge, the orthogonalized update has a spectral norm of approximately one and so the parameter step is bounded by $\eta$ alone. This stabilizes the model for long enough that it picks up a non-trivial fraction of the parsing signal, although not long enough to reach a competitive accuracy.

\paragraph{Switching to pre-norm and adding warmup rescues the Transformer.} After replacing the post-norm residual layout with the pre-norm layout of \citet{xiong2020lnorder} and adding a 500-step linear warmup followed by a cosine decay to ten percent of the peak learning rate, the matched-budget Transformer with Adam reaches 82.00 dev UAS, an increase of 69.80 over the original post-norm Adam configuration. We highlight this number because it isolates a single design defect---post-norm-without-warmup---as the cause of a near-catastrophic accuracy loss, and it shows that the modern fix (pre-norm plus warmup) is sufficient to recover the great majority of the lost accuracy. The same encoder trained with Muon reaches 81.03 dev UAS, but only after we reduce the Muon learning rate from the default 0.02 down to 0.005. At 0.02, the matched-budget Transformer with Muon early-stops at 58.42 dev UAS, a failure mode we trace in Section~\ref{sec:exp-encoder-discussion} to the interaction between the small parameter dimensions of this 9M model and the spectral-norm-one effective step size of Muon updates.

\paragraph{Scaling to 76M parameters does not close the gap to BiLSTM.} We train a 76M-parameter Transformer that integrates pre-norm residuals, RoPE, SwiGLU feed-forward blocks, LayerScale, weight EMA with decay 0.999, and a 1500-step warmup followed by a cosine decay. This is, to our knowledge, a representative configuration of the modern Transformer stack as deployed in current language modelling work. After 100 epochs of training the model reaches 83.92 dev UAS, which is 2.51 UAS below the BiLSTM Muon baseline and 2.51 UAS below the original Stage-1 BiLSTM Adam baseline. We read this result as the clearest evidence the matrix provides for our main thesis: the gap between the modern Transformer stack and the 2017-style BiLSTM is fundamentally a data-scale gap, and it is not closed by an eight-fold increase in parameter count.

Two patterns in the cross-section are worth highlighting. The first is that the Muon-over-Adam advantage is most pronounced precisely in the column where the training would otherwise fail, the post-norm Transformer, and is absent in the column where the training is already stable, the pre-norm Transformer. This is the empirical signature that one would predict from the spectral-norm bound on the orthogonalized update derived in Section~\ref{sec:theory}: when the unconstrained gradient would push the network out of the basin of convergence, projecting the update onto the unit-spectral-norm ball restores stability, but when the unconstrained gradient is already bounded the projection adds no value. The second pattern is that within the column where the training is stable (the pre-norm 9M Transformer), Muon at its default learning rate of 0.02 fails to converge and the configuration that succeeds is the rescaled Muon learning rate of 0.005. This is consistent with the observation that the Muon update has an effective step size of approximately $\eta$ (the spectral norm of the orthogonalized update is approximately one), so that a Muon learning rate must be chosen to be roughly comparable to the effective Adam learning rate of $\eta / \sqrt{\hat{v}}$, which on this 9M Transformer is on the order of $10^{-3}$. The small Transformer is too small to absorb the larger 0.02 effective step that the BiLSTM tolerates.

\subsection{E. SOTA scale-up and the data-scale ceiling}
\label{sec:exp-sota}

The final experiment family asks how far the modern Transformer stack can be scaled before the gains stop materializing on this treebank. The 76M-parameter SOTA configuration of Section~\ref{sec:exp-encoder} is the deepest, widest and most carefully tuned Transformer we trained, and it reaches 83.92 dev UAS after 100 epochs. The learning curve has three distinct phases. During the first 23 epochs the warmup schedule pulls the learning rate up from zero to its peak of 0.02, and the development UAS climbs slowly from 12.7 to about 29. During the next 25 epochs, with the learning rate at or near its peak, the UAS rises rapidly from 29 to 81, capturing the bulk of the available accuracy. During the final 50 epochs the cosine schedule decays the learning rate back to one percent of its peak, and the UAS climbs another 2.9 percentage points to 83.92. The marginal accuracy per epoch in this final phase is roughly one-tenth of the marginal accuracy per epoch in the rapid phase, a textbook signal of an overparameterized model that has already absorbed all the information that the training set can supply.

We interpret the 83.92 UAS as the data-scale ceiling for this treebank under our experimental protocol. The remaining 2.5 UAS gap to the BiLSTM Muon baseline can plausibly be closed by either (a) acquiring an additional order of magnitude of labeled training data, or (b) initializing the encoder from a large-scale pretrained backbone such as BERT-base-Chinese; we did not pursue either route in this work because both are outside the scope of an Adam-vs.-Muon, BiLSTM-vs.-Transformer ablation.

\subsection{F. Negative results we explicitly omit}
\label{sec:exp-negatives}

We briefly document four design directions that we considered for the Stage-2 SOTA configuration and that we ultimately did not pursue, so that future work knows the boundaries of our search. Mixture-of-experts routing on the Transformer feed-forward block would, on a 8K-sentence corpus, exhaust the router-stability budget that has been established to require corpora at least three orders of magnitude larger. Linear-attention substitutes for softmax attention trade representational capacity for sequence-length efficiency, but our average sentence length of 30 tokens makes the trade-off straightforwardly unfavorable. The multi-head causal connections of \citet{deepseekteam2025mhc}, introduced in late 2025 in a setting that uses billions of tokens of unsupervised pretraining, were considered but the trace from that regime to our 8K-sentence supervised treebank is one we cannot calibrate within the time budget of this work. The gated-attention variant of \citet{openreview2025gateattn} is conceptually similar to the LayerScale we already use and we judged it to be a redundant axis. We mention these directions in the negative because each of them has been claimed to work in adjacent settings, and the reader is entitled to know that we evaluated them.
