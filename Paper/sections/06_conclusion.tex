\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We revisited the Biaffine dependency parser of \citet{dozat2017biaffine} on a 8\,301-sentence Chinese treebank and ran a fourteen-configuration ablation matrix that crosses encoder family, optimizer, and pretrained word vector regime. The matrix supports three concise conclusions. The BiLSTM contextual encoder remains the right choice on this data scale, beating an eight-times-larger modern Transformer by 2.5 UAS even when the latter is equipped with the full battery of contemporary stability and capacity tricks. The Muon optimizer reaches a slightly better optimum than Adam on the BiLSTM provided the schedule is extended to 100 epochs with warmup and cosine decay, surpassing the 2017-style Adam baseline by 0.5 UAS. The Newton--Schulz orthogonalization at the core of Muon acts as an implicit gradient clipper that recovers four times the accuracy of Adam in the post-norm Transformer regime, a regime that would otherwise diverge to near-random accuracy.

The pattern that emerges from the matrix is one of compute--data asymmetry. Once the data is fixed, increasing the parameter count of the contextual encoder does not buy what one might expect; the gains that the modern Transformer stack obtains on language modelling and machine translation, where the data scale is several orders of magnitude larger, do not transfer to a 8K-sentence supervised treebank. The optimization frontier, on the other hand, remains open: a better-scheduled Muon optimizer on the same BiLSTM produces a small but reproducible improvement over the 2017 baseline that the field has come to take for granted. We read the combined result as a reminder that the inductive bias of the architecture and the training schedule of the optimizer occupy different parts of the design space, and that progress on small-data structured prediction tasks may come more reliably from the latter than from the former. The training scripts, the auto-routing Muon wrapper, and the preflight checklist that consolidates the failure modes we encountered are released alongside this paper as artifacts that we hope will be of use to others working in similar regimes.
