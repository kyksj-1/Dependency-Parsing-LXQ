\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Dependency parsing assigns each token in a sentence a single \emph{head} token together with a labeled grammatical relation, yielding a rooted tree that exposes predicate--argument structure for downstream NLP. Among the two dominant paradigms, transition-based parsing builds the tree incrementally through a sequence of shift--reduce actions, whereas graph-based parsing scores every candidate head--dependent edge and decodes the maximum-spanning tree (MST) over the resulting score matrix. The \emph{deep biaffine} parser of \citet{dozat2017biaffine} is the canonical graph-based design and remains, almost a decade later, a remarkably hard baseline to beat on mid-size treebanks: a three-layer bidirectional LSTM encodes the sentence into contextualized representations $\bH\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times d}$, two separate single-layer MLPs project each token into a ``head-role'' and a ``dependent-role'' vector, and a bilinear attention module produces an $L\times L$ arc score matrix from which the tree is decoded by an MST algorithm on the predicted scores.

In the eight years since that design was frozen, almost every component of the surrounding ecosystem has changed. Pretrained word vectors are now routinely supplied by 300-dimensional Chinese skip-gram models trained on corpora that are orders of magnitude larger than those available in 2017. Transformer encoders backed by scaled dot-product attention have displaced BiLSTMs as the default contextualizer in virtually every NLP task. A new generation of optimizers, most prominently the Muon optimizer of \citet{jordan2024muon} which performs Newton--Schulz orthogonalization on the momentum buffers of two-dimensional weight matrices, has begun to outperform AdamW on small-scale neural network training. And a long list of stability tricks (pre-norm residuals, rotary positional encodings, SwiGLU feed-forward blocks, LayerScale, weight averaging, warmup--cosine learning-rate schedules) has been validated across the language-modelling and vision literatures.

It is therefore natural to ask whether the design choices that made Biaffine attractive in 2017 still hold today, or whether the modern stack can simply be substituted in for a near-free accuracy gain. This question is non-trivial precisely because dependency treebanks remain expensive to annotate: the Chinese treebank used in our work contains only 8\,301 training sentences, a regime in which the comfortable assumption ``a bigger transformer will always do better'' is far from automatic. In a recent series of works on low-resource structured prediction, the inductive biases that BiLSTMs encode (locality, monotone left-to-right propagation, recency-weighted memory) have repeatedly emerged as competitive with attention-based encoders precisely because the latter must learn similar biases from data they do not have. We therefore set out to perform a clean ablation in which every component of the Biaffine pipeline except the contextual encoder and the optimizer is held fixed, and we treat the encoder choice and the optimizer choice as orthogonal axes whose interaction may be more informative than either axis taken in isolation.

\paragraph{Contributions.} Our contributions are threefold. First, we present a fourteen-configuration training matrix that crosses two encoders (BiLSTM and SDPA Transformer at matched and at scaled-up parameter budgets), three optimizers (Adam, SGD, and Muon) and three families of pretrained word vector resources. The matrix is the largest published controlled ablation we are aware of on this particular treebank. Second, we diagnose two failure modes that arise in this small-data regime---post-norm Transformers without warmup that collapse to near-random accuracy, and the surprising mismatch between Muon's default learning rate and small Transformer encoders---and document them with the kind of detail that would have allowed us to avoid them ourselves. Third, we report a concrete empirical conclusion: in this 8.3K-sentence regime, the BiLSTM trained for one hundred epochs with Muon under a warmup--cosine schedule surpasses the 2017-style Adam baseline (86.93 vs.\ 86.43 UAS), while a 76M-parameter modern Transformer equipped with the full battery of contemporary tricks reaches only 83.92 UAS---a result we read as evidence that the inductive bias of recurrence remains genuinely useful when data is the scarce resource and parameters are not.
