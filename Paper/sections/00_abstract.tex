\begin{abstract}
We revisit the \emph{Deep Biaffine Attention} parser of \citet{dozat2017biaffine} on a 8.3K-sentence Chinese dependency treebank and ask a question that the original paper could not answer in 2017: now that we have all the modern Transformer tricks at our disposal, does Biaffine parsing still want a BiLSTM encoder? We run a fourteen-configuration matrix that crosses three pretrained word vector regimes, three optimizers (Adam, SGD, and Muon \citep{jordan2024muon}), and three encoder families (the original three-layer BiLSTM, a same-budget Transformer with sinusoidal positional encoding, and a 76M-parameter Transformer that stacks RoPE \citep{su2021roformer}, SwiGLU \citep{shazeer2020glu}, LayerScale \citep{touvron2021cait}, pre-norm \citep{xiong2020lnorder}, weight EMA and a warmup--cosine schedule). The cleanest finding is that the inductive biases that motivated Dozat's original choice still win in this data regime: our best Transformer reaches only 83.92 UAS, while the BiLSTM trained with Muon for one hundred epochs reaches 86.93 UAS and surpasses the Adam baseline of 86.43 by a small but reproducible margin. Along the way we diagnose a textbook failure mode of post-norm Transformers without warmup that initially collapsed our SDPA runs to near-random accuracy, and show that Muon's Newton--Schulz orthogonalization step acts as an implicit gradient clipper that recovers four times the accuracy of Adam in exactly this unstable regime. We release all training scripts, the 1\,M-parameter Muon optimizer wrapper that auto-routes embeddings and biases to AdamW, and a checklist of preflight items that would have prevented our own failures.
\end{abstract}
